In addition to my ongoing blog at https://datafox.tw (feel free to keep following my blog!), I've also started doing something else more consciously:
Continuously sharing my observations, things I've learned, and the detours and corrections made along the way.

I remember first conceptualizing my podcast content near the end of my exchange program, brainstorming while riding a scooter to Oia on Santorini, and stopping by the roadside to jot down a good topic when it came to me. Although the busyness of my master's studies and course scheduling in the second half of 2025 meant I didn't actively participate in the NTU Podcast Club, which slightly demotivated me, I've since received a decent scholarship and decided to use a portion of it to buy podcast equipment. And when I considered that if I ever gave up, I could sell the equipment I bought and recoup about 70% of the cost, I felt it was a worthwhile investment.

I hope to leave a legacy. Besides the written word I'm accustomed to, I also want to use audio to leave my mark, allowing people to understand me and my excellent partners in a new way, and to share our stories with everyone.

For me, this isn't just content creation; it's a process of "building a personal brand." It's not about packaging myself as incredibly impressive, but rather hoping that someday, when someone is feeling lost, they might come across these records and think—"Ah, so someone else has walked this path too."

So, I've embarked on a new endeavor:
🎙️ The "Life Beta" Podcast!

This show will interview friends around 22–24 years old, who have recently finished university or just entered the workforce, discussing: the main quests they initially thought they had, the accidental side quests, and the turning points only understood in retrospect. Each episode will be an audio letter written to their "confused past self."

If you're also exploring life, or simply want to hear stories that aren't about typical success, welcome to be an early listener (I'd be very grateful for you to join me in beta testing 🙏)

If you:
- Feel your life is a bit chaotic but actually quite exciting
- Or have just walked a path worth looking back on

## 🎙️ Core Equipment: Why This Setup?

The current configuration is very smart, especially the **Samson Q2U**, this "miracle mic," which combines USB and XLR interfaces, making it the top choice for those with limited budgets. Paired with the industry-standard **Scarlett 2i2** audio interface, this combination can produce near-professional broadcast quality audio.

### 1. Microphone: Samson Q2U (Dynamic)
* **Its characteristic:** It's like an ear that "only hears close-up sounds." It's very effective at blocking ambient noise (AC hum, outside traffic sounds).
* **Key operation:** Because it's a dynamic microphone, the signal is relatively weak. When recording, **be sure to hold the microphone close to your mouth (about 5–10 cm, a fist's width away)** for a rich, full sound.
* **Note:** Since you have an audio interface, **only plug in the XLR cable** (the thick, round-headed cable). Never plug in both USB and XLR simultaneously, as this will cause signal interference.

### 2. Audio Interface: Scarlett 2i2 (3rd Gen)
* **Its role:** It's the "translator" for audio entering the computer.
* **A small quirk you might encounter:** You might find yourself turning the Gain knob almost all the way up for the sound to be loud enough. This is normal; as long as the background noise (hiss) doesn't affect the listening experience, it's fine.
* **Future upgrade path:** If you later find the sound is still too low, you can add a **Cloudlifter (mic preamplifier)**, which provides "clean boost" and reduces the strain on the audio interface.

## 🎧 Monitoring and Oversight: The Feel of Recording in the Moment

### Headphone Solution for Two-Person Recording
You are currently using "wired headphones + adapters."
* **Potential concern:** The volume of the two headphones might be uneven, or different impedances could lead to discrepancies in what's heard.
* **Recommended optimization:** Buy an inexpensive **Headphone Splitter (multi-output headphone amplifier)**. This way, both you and your guest can adjust your headphone volumes independently, significantly improving the recording experience.

### Maximize Hardware Monitoring (Direct Monitor)
* **Do not** monitor through Audacity software, because sound processed by the computer will have a slight delay (latency), and hearing your own voice 0.1 seconds late will make you stutter.
* **Do** press the **Direct Monitor** button on the 2i2 to listen directly to the sound returned by the audio interface; this is the true, zero-latency feedback.

## 💻 Software Settings: Audacity's Golden Rules

Before pressing the record button, please confirm these settings:

* **Sample Rate:** Set permanently to **48kHz** (the mainstream industry standard for podcasts).
* **Bit Depth:** When recording, choose **24-bit** (to preserve more headroom for editing), then export as 16-bit for saving.
* **Recording Level (Input Level):**
    * **Average value:** Should fall between **-18 to -12 dB**.
    * **Peak value:** Absolutely must not exceed **-6 dB**.
    * **Fatal flaw:** If the waveform hits the top (0 dB), the sound will be distorted (Clipping), which is a disaster that cannot be fixed by post-production software.

## 🔊 Decisive Factors for Results: Priority Ranking

If you have limited energy to improve audio quality, prioritize improvements in this order:

| Rank | Item | Impact | Execution Advice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Microphone Distance** | **50%** | **Most important!** Get close to the microphone, and the audio quality instantly becomes premium. |
| **2** | **Environmental Reflection** | **30%** | Don't record in an overly spacious room; add some pillows or hang clothes for sound absorption to reduce echo. |
| **3** | **Gain Staging** | **15%** | Adjust the recording level properly, ensuring the sound is loud enough and doesn't clip. |
| **4** | **Equipment Difference** | **5%** | In fact, if the first three items are done correctly, equipment worth a few thousand can achieve the effect of tens of thousands. |
