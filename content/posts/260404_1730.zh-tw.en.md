TITLE: Observations on the Taipei Mayoral Election (with Data to Follow)
DESCRIPTION: This article offers an in-depth look at the Taipei Mayoral Election, analyzing the electoral difficulties Shen Po-yang faces among swing voters after his image shifted from a professional one to a "Resist China, Defend Taiwan" party label. It also examines how Chiang Wan-an skillfully employed a "hands-off" (apolitical) strategy to consolidate the city's governance foundation and his personal favorability with a stable demeanor. The article further explores the DPP's long-term operational challenges in Taipei and assesses the possible future direction of the election and the key factors for victory or defeat.
BODY:

I feel that until early 2024, Shen Po-yang's public image was predominantly positive. Especially when "The Night Show" invited Shen to introduce his expertise at the end of 2023, and when he published his book "What If the Commies Attack?" at that time, he was seen as a normal person, a professional in his field. At that point, "Resist China, Defend Taiwan" was a quite reasonable stance, because the party affiliation wasn't as strong then. People, especially the youth, tended to trust his professional ability more than his party affiliation.

However, ever since he became a legislator and was used as a "DPP's Resist China, Defend Taiwan" symbol/shield by the DPP when they tried to launch various movements from 2024 until now, widespread attacks and magnified misstatements by the media have firmly affixed the label "DPP's dog" onto him. At this point, even if he tries to emphasize his professional attributes, his strong party affiliation will scare away so-called swing voters. Although they might try to engage with his professionalism, his strong party affiliation will overshadow it, cementing an image like "Bluebird/DPP lapdog" onto him. At this point, it would be an unwinnable battle for him to represent the party in the Taipei mayoral election. Chiang Wan-an is very shrewd; he handles city governance very cleverly. He doesn't actively stir up trouble, nor does he get too close to so-called party elders or pan-red/pro-unification factions. He subtly leverages his status as a descendant of the Chiang family, trying to steer public perception away from his direct relationship with Chiang Kai-shek. Instead, he adopts a flexible, almost "hands-off" approach to governance, and then emerges to reap policy benefits when city projects are completed smoothly. Having neither much positive nor much negative feedback is already the best outcome.

Therefore, even if Chiang's personal ability and popularity are not outstanding (he's the kind of person who says whatever the media tells him to, good at packaging but also quite boring), he can still leverage policy dividends and the goodwill he has accumulated (a handsome face + low presence and no controversy) to play defense very easily. Against Shen, with his DPP and "Resist China, Defend Taiwan" attributes, as long as the KMT frames Shen as a loudly squealing rat crossing the street, Chiang's position is essentially very stable—to the extent that even if Chiang committed a crime and was suspended, he would still be elected.
Finally, the attributes of "Resist China, Defend Taiwan," GIS positioning, and war game expert should really be prioritized for political appointments in the Executive Yuan, second for the Legislative Yuan. The worst option is for a capital city mayor. Furthermore, from Ko Wen-je in 2014, then pushing Pasuya Yao in 2018 while maintaining an ambiguous relationship with Ko, to Chen Shih-chung in 2022—none of these were characters the DPP had cultivated long-term in Taipei. If, in 2026, they continue not to nominate a choice with genuine long-term local influence in ground combat (electioneering), the election will only continue to be disadvantaged, unable to change the status quo of non-green parties continuously governing since 1998.

Civilian defense is important, but for conscripted men, most feel "I don't want to serve extra military time, damn it!" Or human instinct makes them feel no need for wartime knowledge (they'll deal with it when it actually happens), so promoting civilian defense awareness really isn't effective (or rather, Taiwan is genuinely too comfortable and has good public safety, though all Earthlings tend to feel their own country's public safety and economy aren't good enough).

Then Shen again talked about conceptual terms like "Fifth Column" instead of things actually happening around people, making it feel like a form of anxiety-mongering (but I also feel Taiwanese generally lack a sense of crisis; PLA aircraft circling Taiwan or submarine cable disruptions are continuously happening, but Taiwanese feel if it doesn't affect them personally, it's all good), so promoting these ideas to the public really faces many obstacles.

I think randomly picking any incumbent legislator (but none of them want to) is the best option. Then choosing someone like a Taipei City Councilor with two or more terms, or an unsuccessful legislative candidate (no news from them) — only then would they look for someone not born in Taipei. But those with a real electoral base, who have been vetted, simply don't want to be mayor in Taipei. I think any of the green camp representatives from Taipei's eight legislative districts at the time (except Miao Poya) would be better than "Black Bear" (referring to Shen Po-yang).

Now some even suggest Tsai Ing-wen should run (she wouldn't want to), as she's the only one with a chance to win. Then there's Chen Chien-jen, but he seems to prefer researching medicine at Academia Sinica. Then Chen Chi-mai, but his "warm man mayor" label from Kaohsiung can't be directly transferred to Taipei.

The DPP just thinks, "Let's find someone with high public visibility; anyone will do as long as they seem green enough." Although some mention Alyssa Chia or Hsieh Chen-wu, I think these are just supporters making random matches. This year will probably be a miserable loss. It remains to be seen if the DPP is willing to find someone in Taipei like Su Chiao-hui in New Taipei, who says, "I want to run, and I want to win," and cultivate them over four years.

The Taipei mayor probably needs a few characteristics:
1. Presentable
2. Efficient
3. Looks very promising
But it's hard for the DPP to find someone who meets at least two of these conditions simultaneously, let alone someone willing to fight for it. For example, Cheng Li-chun is presentable and promising, but she herself doesn't want to (she has always worked on diplomatic affairs in the Executive Yuan, and it shouldn't be her). Wang Shih-chien is efficient and looks promising, but not presentable. He himself isn't interested. Enoch Wu probably only has presentability, but neither the KMT nor the DPP likes him. His remarks make politics seem like an interesting game, not a battlefield. Now some are pushing Chen Chi-mai; he is actually close to the best option, but he just finished his term as Kaohsiung mayor. Although he is more dynamic and has a story, he himself doesn't seem to have the intention.

Last rant: In short, I really think Shen is the worst option I can imagine. His appearance would even affect the entire national election, being labeled as "the DPP loves war." Although I dislike the blue and white camps, I also don't want the party dedicated to safeguarding Taiwan's sovereignty to mess things up like this.
