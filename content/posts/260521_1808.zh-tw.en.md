At that time, I felt that the Decoder, at its core, was merely performing "text completion" based on the input, without being able to foresee the overall generation. But looking back now, if we don't blindly join the hype of AI sycophants, spouting pretty words like "because GPT has more soul," but instead coolly dissect it from the underlying principles of Information Theory and engineering realities, you'll find that the triumph of Decoder-only models is simply an inevitable outcome dictated by mathematical and physical constraints.

## 1. The Brutal Aesthetics of a Unified Architecture: Cross-Entropy and the Price of Being Too Clever

While Seq2Seq has clear logic, it separates "understanding" and "generation" into two modules, which, in terms of its mathematical objective function, essentially "self-sabotages" the purity of information compression.

Let's return to the purest definition in probability and information theory—the **Chain Rule for Entropy**:

$$H(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} H(X_i | X_{i-1}, \dots, X_1)$$

This formula tells us that to quantify the total uncertainty of a piece of human language, the most perfect and lossless way to decompose it is to calculate and sum up "the conditional entropy of the next token, given all historical information."

And the Next-Token Prediction that LLMs perform daily, its loss function is essentially minimizing the **Cross-Entropy** between the real-world human language distribution $p$ and the model's predicted distribution $q$:

$$H(p, q) = H(p) + D_{\text{KL}}(p \parallel q)$$

The physical meaning here is profoundly elegant: $H(p)$ is "God's entropy," the ultimate inherent randomness of human language itself; and the KL divergence $D_{\text{KL}}(p \parallel q)$ is the average additional informational cost (noise) that the model incurs at each step during completion because it's not "smart" enough. The ultimate goal of optimizing the model is to push this cost towards 0. In contrast, Seq2Seq insists on splitting this unified optimization task, using bidirectional attention for Span Corruption (fill-in-the-blank), and then using Cross-Attention to retrieve embeddings. This shatters the conditional entropy chain rule that quantifies everything, making the optimization objective in the parameter space extremely impure.

## 2. A Challenge: Do We Really Want KL Divergence to Go to Zero?

Okay, at this point, if someone were to raise their hand and ask: "According to you, we want $D_{\text{KL}}$ to be as low as possible, so isn't the ultimate goal to make it equal to 0? If the model's distribution is exactly the same as the true human data samples, wouldn't it just be a random parrot that memorizes everything? Where exactly do these 'emergent capabilities' you constantly talk about come from?" This very challenge is the ultimate key to unlocking the mystery of the Decoder-only triumph.

**The truth is: Precisely because of the limitations of physical reality, emergence is actually a byproduct of "lossy compression."**

The knowledge space, physical laws, and causal logic (the immense $H(p)$) behind human language are almost infinite. However, the model's parameters (whether 7B or hundreds of billions of parameters) are extremely limited in the hardware world. Statistically, it is **absolutely impossible** for the model to truly make the KL divergence zero.

In such a desperate situation—with limited capacity yet forced to push cross-entropy to its minimum—the model discovers that rote memorization simply isn't feasible. Its only path to achieving a high score is to be forced, within its internal neurons, to abstract out the world's underlying operational rules, logical syntax, and causal World Model.

## 3. Why Does "Fluent Completion" Equate to "Understanding the World"?

Many people believe Decoder-only models are merely playing a shallow game of probabilistic text completion, wondering how they could possibly understand physics or programming.

Let's conduct a thought experiment. Consider this sentence:
> "A 100 kg person and a 50 kg person go down a slide, the time theoretically required is \underline{\hspace{1cm}}."

For the model to accurately complete with "the same" (一樣), thereby lowering its conditional entropy, relying solely on statistical word frequency would definitely lead to failure. Through the extreme compression and refinement of trillions of tokens, it is forced to implicitly simulate concepts like "gravity" and "friction."

This is the extraordinary realm that Decoder-only models achieve through pure Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE). It seemingly just pursues fluency in generating text, but because the causal logic behind human language is so rigorous, to achieve ultimate coherence, it is forced to become a simulator of this world. Perhaps "understanding" was never the goal, but rather a byproduct generated for the sake of "accurate completion."

## Conclusion: How Far Are We From AGI?

While everyone debates how AGI will be achieved, observing the current operational mode of GPT: first, it internally computes (thinks) what to output via Prefix LM, and then, upon starting output, it enters a self-regressive state akin to "text completion." Upon reflection, this aligns quite well with human behavior. After all, once we've formulated what we want to say in our minds, the subsequent process of speaking often becomes a deterministic chain. Although humans continuously "think first, then speak, then revise" during conversation, this fundamentally differs from current LLMs that generate content in a single pass.

Nevertheless, it is undeniable that the Decoder-only architecture, with its extreme scalability, pure mathematical objective, and computational efficiency, has indeed led us to unprecedented heights in AI.
