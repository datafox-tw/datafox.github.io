I've recently been looking back at the news from 2023, when everyone was touting prompt engineers as the next high-paying opportunity in Silicon Valley, or even the best career path for liberal arts majors to enter the tech industry. For example, this article: [link](https://www.managertoday.com.tw/articles/view/66814)

It was after seeing such content that I decided to try transitioning from the financial industry to the AI field, and to my surprise, I found myself getting more and more deeply involved.

Looking back at the summer of 2023, it was the year when "Prompt Engineers" were elevated to a god-like status. At that time, watching the process of Midjourney's early days, which required extremely precise, almost mystical keywords to produce usable images, I once thought: perhaps this is the future—an art where humans use "intuition" and "experience" to probe the model's boundaries. Through accumulated human experience, we would find the "correct way to open" this black box, understanding how to awaken this large magic box to get what we needed, all while dealing with significant variance.

But standing here today in 2026, this myth has been completely shattered.

1. A Good Prompt Isn't Talent, But a Model's "Deficiency"

Back then, we thought that being able to write good prompts was a rare talent. Now, it appears to be merely compensatory behavior resulting from an incomplete model training distribution. I've recently been re-reading CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training), which is a golden standard for combining images and natural language and remains useful even five years later. However, some tricks are now considered model deficiencies, and these deficiencies were already mentioned in the original papers as issues to be addressed through the evolution of model architecture, rather than prompt engineering. At the time, prompt engineering felt more like a reluctant measure to optimize performance.

In the early stages of architecture, the model's understanding of semantics was full of noise. The reason we had to write "a photo of...", "4k resolution", or "hyper-realistic" was essentially to manually "calibrate features" for the model. We weren't creating; we were helping the model find the few correctly labeled coordinate points within that distorted high-dimensional manifold.

With the explosive breakthroughs in computational power and advancements in data cleaning techniques, models now possess powerful semantic alignment capabilities. When a model can precisely understand "natural language," those deliberately stacked "keyword incantations" become worthless. As models get smarter, human "tuning experience" depreciates in value.

2. Back to Basics: Why is Underlying Architecture the Truth?

When "spells" are no longer mysterious, the true moats become apparent. Rather than delving into "how to talk to the model," it's better to understand "why the model talks that way." For example:
- Next-token Generation: Understand the nature of autoregressive models, and you'll realize why models hallucinate and why "CoT" (Chain-of-Thought) can improve logic.
- Bi-encoder and Cross-encoder: Understand the alignment and interaction of vector spaces, and you'll know why some retrieval tasks (RAG) are never perfectly accurate, and why CLIP can achieve zero-shot learning.

This understanding of underlying architectures is the hard knowledge that transcends model iteration cycles. When you grasp the underlying logic, you won't feel anxious when Midjourney releases V7 or Gemini releases a new version, because you know that no matter how the surface changes, the mathematical essence remains the same.

Furthermore, Professor Hung-yi Lee's courses also strive to identify content that will remain crucial for years to come in this era of rapid development, and teach it to everyone. I believe this embodies the spirit that a true professional should possess, and I am grateful for his videos over the past few years, which have provided us with correct intuitions about LLMs.

3. The Collective Anxiety of Startups: Your Feature Is Just Someone Else's "By the Way"

This also serves as a huge warning for job seekers and entrepreneurs. In early 2024, countless startups invested significant effort in developing prompt-based applications (e.g., helping you write resumes, editing your images), or even connecting APIs to analyze financial information. What happened? When Claude or Gemini updated a small feature, these startups were often "dimensionally reduced" within a single day.

Because if your moat is built upon others' "imperfections," when others become perfect, you simply vanish.

4. The Future's "Golden Combination": Underlying Understanding + Domain Expertise

So, in 2026, what kind of talent will truly be valuable?
The answer is: individuals who possess a deep understanding of underlying AI architectures and can combine it with profound domain knowledge.

If you understand law and also how AI retrieves case precedents through vector spaces, you can develop tools that professional lawyers would genuinely trust and use.

If you (truly) understand finance and recognize the limitations of Transformers in processing time series and structured data, you won't blindly trust the predictions models give.
A technological moat is never about the cutting-edge, easily superseded "trendy knowledge," but rather a deep coupling of underlying logic with real-world professional domains. This combination is the moat that brute-force computation cannot easily overcome.
