TITLE: Why Are Today's LLMs Decoder-only? Architectural Evolution and Reflections from Seq2Seq to GPT
DESCRIPTION: When the Transformer first emerged, the Seq2Seq architecture seemed more reasonable. But why have all mainstream LLMs (like GPT) now adopted a Decoder-only approach? This article explores the differences between Decoder and Seq2Seq architectures, and the connections between Next Token Prediction, Scaling Law, and model emergent abilities.
BODY:

"If we are on the path to a Turing machine, Seq2Seq itself is more reasonable than a Decoder." This is a sentence I wrote in my notes in 2022.

Back then, when I played around with early GPT-2, I always thought: what exactly *is* this thing? In comparison, BART or T5, based on the Seq2Seq concept, seemed much more reasonable. Unexpectedly, a few years later, in this AI arms race, the **Decoder-only** architecture has emerged as the MVP.

At the time, I felt that a Decoder, at its core, was merely playing a "word chain game" based on the input. It couldn't pre-plan what it was going to generate overall; it could only continuously select from the Sample Space (樣本空間) "which next token has the highest probability in the current context."

In contrast, **Seq2Seq** was the "good student" that fully utilized the Transformer architecture:
1. **Encoder** is responsible for understanding the entire sentence structure and generating embeddings rich in semantic details.
2. **Decoder** applies the global context (Context) provided by the Encoder to output sequences.

This division of labor sounds incredibly reasonable, right? But why has the current development completely shifted towards Decoder-only? Below are my subsequent reflections, along with several key reasons compiled after recent discussions about AI.

---

## 1. The Brute-Force Elegance of a Unified Architecture: Scaling Law and Objective Functions

Although Seq2Seq has clear logic, it separates "understanding" and "generation" into two modules. The Encoder is responsible for understanding and producing embeddings for detailed concepts. This, in fact, incurred a hidden cost: **inconsistent training objectives**.

Seq2Seq often uses **Span Corruption** (for example, blanking out a segment of text and having the AI fill in the blanks, similar to BERT/T5's approach) for pre-training; whereas GPT consistently uses **Next Token Prediction (predicting the next token)**.

Subsequent facts have proven that when model parameter scales expand to tens of billions or hundreds of billions, Next Token Prediction, which adheres to the **Scaling Law (規模法則)**, is the "objective function" best capable of extracting value from data. When we aim to minimize the Loss Function and make Decoder-only models perform increasingly better, because they must predict the next token accurately enough, the models are **forced to "simulate" internally the logic, common sense, and even physical laws of the entire world**.

> If it doesn't understand "gravity," it won't be able to accurately append "fall down" after the phrase "After the apple detaches from the branch, it will..."

Perhaps "understanding" was never the goal, but rather a byproduct of "predicting accurately." In other words, although we didn't specifically ask the AI to "thoroughly understand the entire sentence content and output an equal-length sequence or classification label" when training BERT or RoBERTa, the Decoder internally accomplishes this during the process of predicting text. In contrast, no matter how well an Encoder understands, it cannot independently learn how to "write" content.

## 2. "Prefix LM" Blurs the Boundaries of the Encoder

Earlier, I mentioned that "the Encoder can understand the entire sentence structure and see the global context," but this advantage has been replaced by the form of **Prefix LM** in modern Decoder-only models.

Although current LLMs are Decoder-only, when you input a 2000-character Prompt (提示詞), these 2000 characters actually undergo **Full Attention** (during the preparation phase for answering, characters within the Prompt can all see each other's context). Only from the moment it "starts generating the answer" does it switch back to a strict **Causal Mask (因果遮罩)**, meaning it can only see preceding tokens.

It completes both "understanding the global Prompt" and "autoregressively generating the answer" within the same parameter space, which is more flexible and efficient than forcibly splitting parameters into two sets for Encoder and Decoder.

## 3. Training Efficiency and the Economics of GPU Concurrency

From an engineering and computational power perspective, this point is very realistic. We can observe that in the retrieval domain, Bi-encoders brutally outperform Cross-encoders in efficiency. Similarly, when applied to Seq2Seq:

During training, the Seq2Seq Encoder can indeed see the full text, but the computational complexity and data transfer volume of **Cross-Attention** between the Encoder and Decoder become a severe performance bottleneck during distributed training (Distributed Training).

In contrast, **Decoder-only training**:
Since it only uses a Causal Mask, its computation matrix is a very elegant "lower triangular matrix" (when predicting the $t$-th token, it only sees tokens from $1$ to $t-1$). On existing GPU architectures, this pure mechanism can achieve the highest throughput (Throughput) and maximize GPU performance. In an era where training often requires tens of thousands of GPUs, this architectural computational advantage directly determines life or death.

## 4. The Great Zero-shot Emergent Abilities

The Decoder-only architecture has demonstrated extremely strong **In-context Learning (上下文學習)** capabilities.

Early Seq2Seq models (like T5), when faced with unseen task instructions, typically required **Fine-tuning (微調)** to perform well. However, a "word chain machine" like GPT, having seen countless "question: answer" dialogue patterns in its vast pre-training data, can directly treat your Prompt as the first half of the chain it needs to complete, thereby achieving astonishing generalization capabilities (Zero-shot / Few-shot).

This mode of operation is actually quite reasonable for the human brain as well. Although the amount of data required for LLM training is astonishingly large compared to the human brain, the logic of "naturally inferring the next step based on context" is, surprisingly, closer to human intuition.

---

## Conclusion: How Far Are We From AGI?

While everyone debates how AGI will be achieved, observing GPT's current operational mode – first internally computing (thinking) what to output via Prefix LM, and then entering an autoregressive "word chain game" state when it starts generating – is actually quite consistent with human behavior when you think about it.

After all, once we've thought through what we want to say in our minds, the subsequent speaking process often becomes a **Deterministic Chain (決定性鏈條)**. However, humans continuously "think first, then speak, then revise" during the act of speaking... This still fundamentally differs from current LLMs that simply generate once.

Nevertheless, it is undeniable that the Decoder-only architecture, with its extreme scalability and efficiency, has indeed brought us to new heights in AI that were previously unimaginable.
