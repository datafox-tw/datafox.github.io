TITLE: Career Fair Reflections and NTU AI
DESCRIPTION: This article shares the author's passion for software engineering and AI, emphasizing the importance of interdisciplinary learning and multi-model thinking in the current technological wave. Whether you have a liberal arts background or feel lost regarding AI, the author encourages you to leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to lower the barrier to exploration. The article concludes with a detailed introduction to the NTU AI Club, offering a comprehensive AI technology learning roadmap to help you build foundational skills, master ML and LLM applications, and even secure internship opportunities.
BODY:

Although some refer to Taiwan as a "tech-obsessed island," at least, software engineering and AI happen to be exactly what I enjoy. I consider myself very fortunate.

It's undeniable that the global trend is indeed becoming increasingly STEM-centric. Even so, I still deeply respect and continuously engage with knowledge from the humanities. History and philosophy have always been subjects I enjoy. Recently, I even often listen to "Dream of the Red Chamber" as a bedtime story.

After becoming an engineer, I don't want to fixate solely on a single area of technical knowledge. I hope to continuously engage in interdisciplinary learning, understanding the world through Charlie Munger's favorite multi-model thinking. I also aim to use the resources and abilities I acquire in this field to influence those around me, and even bring about a small, positive change in society.

To some extent, I also believe that the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) is lowering the barrier to interdisciplinary exploration.

If you come from a liberal arts background, or have ever felt that you might be left behind by the wave of technology, I genuinely recommend trying to start a conversation with AI. Perhaps by just asking some questions or conducting small experiments, you might unexpectedly discover an interest in engineering, technology, or data analysis. Of course, I'll also continue to pester my LLM with a plethora of imaginative questions, such as existentialism or the history of Gdańsk.

Finally, if you're somewhat interested but feel overwhelmed by current AI developments, please don't hesitate to join the NTU AI Club (National Taiwan University Artificial Intelligence Application Club).

This year, we've designed a comprehensive AI technology roadmap, hoping to help more people understand AI applications, build foundational skills, and learn how to collaborate and learn with AI tools. With just a little practice and curiosity, you might find yourself standing at the doorstep of the ML, LLM, or AI Agent world. This could even lead to opportunities for ML, LLM, or AI Agent internships 🦊😀 (Seriously, this is one of the key goals of our courses.) (I will be teaching ten classes.)
