Many people ask me: "datafox, what exactly do we talk about when I book a Coffee Chat with you?" It's not just a one-way consultation; it's a "stress test" for career hypotheses. Recently, I had a conversation with a student currently pursuing a master's degree in the UK and considering returning to Taiwan for development.

Below is a summary of the highlights from our conversation, along with my genuine perspectives on these topics.

## 1. Cross-Domain Career Transition: Don't Impose Others' Scripts on Yourself

During the conversation, we discussed the case of "medical background transitioning to AI" (which is actually the excellent story of another friend, Iressa).

*   **My Take:** Career transition isn't just about learning new tools; it's about building a completely different "skillset."
*   **Key Reminder:** Many people feel anxious looking at online success stories, but you must clearly distinguish: what are your core competencies? What is merely someone else's halo? For instance, my transition from finance to computer science relied on rebuilding underlying logic, not just rote problem-solving.

## 2. Demystifying the Google Interview Process: Facts Over Rumors

There are many misconceptions about Google's internship interviews. We clarified several key points during our chat:

*   **Process Breakdown:** From resume screening, HR behavioral interviews, online coding tests to the most crucial **Team Match**.
*   **Key to Getting Hired:** Many people believe that a single phone call from a manager during the "Background Check" can determine admission. **This is incorrect.** At Google, your interview performance and fit with the team are central. Background checks are for verifying authenticity, not for replacing interview evaluations.
*   **Internship Quality:** I shared my experience participating in the "Generative AI Customer Service System" at Cathay United Bank. The focus isn't on the company's reputation, but whether you can clearly articulate your specific contributions (e.g., agent training or architecture optimization), rather than exaggerating.

## 3. Coding Interviews: Prohibit "Blind Use of AI", Not AI Itself

This is something I particularly emphasized during our conversation. It's the age of AI, so interviewers naturally know you'll use tools.

*   **The Real Test:** Interviewers don't care if you can write code, but whether you can explain **time and space complexity** and why you chose a particular algorithm.
*   **Beware of Mindless Dependence:** If, during your preparation, you blindly ask AI for answers without contemplating the underlying reasoning, the moment the question is slightly altered in the interview, and your logic breaks, is when you're out.

## 4. UK vs. Taiwan: The Harsh Reality of the Job Market Ecosystem

The interviewee shared the employment challenges faced by UK master's graduates (such as his brother at Durham University).

*   **Market Differences:** The tech industry ecosystems in the UK and Taiwan are vastly different. Taiwan boasts strong hardware integration and emerging AI application demands (e.g., VCs' eagerness for technological innovation discernment).
*   **Networking:** A Coffee Chat isn't for obtaining a recommendation letter (Referral); it's for gaining "first-hand information." Proactively contacting seniors/alumni and understanding a team's real pain points is far more effective than haphazardly sending resumes on LinkedIn.
